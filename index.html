People also ask: 100 bouyon music questions

The basics

What is bouyon music?

Bouyon is a high-energy music genre from Dominica. It blends jing ping, cadence-lypso and traditional dances such as bele, quadrille, chante mas and lapo kabwit with modern electronic production.

Read more : What is bouyon music

Where does bouyon music come from?

Bouyon comes from Roseau, Dominica, where it was created in the late 1980s. It is the island's signature carnival sound.

Read more : Origin of bouyon

Who created bouyon music?

The band WCK (Windward Caribbean Kulture), formed in 1988, pioneered bouyon by fusing jing ping with cadence-lypso. Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade were the other founding groups.

Read more : WCK, the bouyon pioneers

What does the word bouyon mean?

In Antillean Creole, bouyon means a soup or gumbo: many ingredients cooked in one pot. The music takes its name from that mix of styles in a single sound.

Read more : Meaning of bouyon

How do you pronounce bouyon?

Bouyon is pronounced boo-yon, like the French word for broth. In Dominica's Kweyol it names both the soup and the music.

Read more : Meaning of bouyon

What language is bouyon music sung in?

Bouyon is sung mainly in Kweyol, Dominica's French-based Creole, with English and French mixing in as the genre travels through Guadeloupe, Martinique and the diaspora.

Read more : Bouyon and Kweyol

Is bouyon music from Dominica or the Dominican Republic?

Dominica, the small English and Kweyol speaking island in the Lesser Antilles, not the Dominican Republic. The two are different countries, and bouyon is Dominican (Dominica) music.

Read more : Bouyon in Dominica

Why is bouyon called soup?

Because like a bouyon soup, the genre cooks many ingredients together: jing ping, cadence-lypso, bele, quadrille, chante mas, lapo kabwit, mazurka and zouk, all in one pot.

Read more : Meaning of bouyon

What is bouyon music also called?

In Guadeloupe and Martinique bouyon is commonly called jump up music. Its fusions carry their own names too: bouyon soca, bouyon gwada, bouyon-muffin and reketeng.

Read more : Jump up

Is bouyon a type of soca?

No. Bouyon is its own Dominican genre. It shares carnival DNA with soca, and the two fuse as bouyon soca, but bouyon's jing ping and cadence-lypso roots make it a different sound.

Read more : Bouyon vs soca

Who is the King of Bouyon?

Asa Bantan, real name Asa Edwards, from Grand Bay in Dominica, is known as the King of Bouyon and the Bouyon Boss. He is also the name behind Dominica's Breakfast Fete.

Read more : Asa Bantan

Who is the Prince of Bouyon?

Reo is billed as the Prince of Bouyon, a CMA 2026 nominee carrying the new generation of Dominica's sound, heard for example on Joli BB with Maurane Voyer.

Read more : Reo

What is the national music of Dominica?

Dominica's signature sounds are cadence-lypso and its faster descendant, bouyon. Jing ping is the island's traditional folk band music, played on accordion, banjo and percussion.

Read more : Bouyon in Dominica

Is bouyon popular outside Dominica?

Yes. Bouyon is huge in Guadeloupe and Martinique, strong in Saint Lucia, French Guiana and Saint Martin, and growing in Paris and the Caribbean diaspora through diaspora parties and social media.

Read more : Bouyon in the diaspora

What is the bouyon flag emoji combo fans use?

Fans pair the Dominica flag with rocket and globe emojis, as in the Bouyon Shattaz rallying tag #bouyontotheworld, a push to carry Dominica's sound worldwide.

Read more : Bouyon Shattaz

What does bouyon sound like?

Fast drums, heavy bass, bright keyboard riffs and call and response Kweyol vocals at carnival tempo. If soca is a parade, bouyon is the truck everyone is jumping behind.

Read more : What is bouyon music

Is bouyon the same as jump up?

In Guadeloupe and Martinique, yes: jump up is the local name for bouyon music. In Dominica it is simply bouyon.

Read more : Jump up

History and roots

When did bouyon music start?

Bouyon started in the late 1980s in Roseau, Dominica. WCK formed in 1988 and built the first bouyon sound from jing ping and cadence-lypso.

Read more : Origin of bouyon

What is jing ping?

Jing ping is Dominica's traditional folk music, played by bands with accordion, banjo, shak shak, gwaj and boom boom. It is one of bouyon's two parent sounds.

Read more : Jing ping

What is cadence-lypso?

Cadence-lypso is the Dominican genre of the 1970s that fused Haitian cadence rampa with calypso, led by Exile One. It is bouyon's other parent sound.

Read more : Cadence-lypso

What is WCK?

WCK stands for Windward Caribbean Kulture, the Roseau band formed in 1988 that created bouyon. They are recognised as the Bouyon Pioneers.

Read more : WCK

Who were the first bouyon bands?

The founding bouyon groups were WCK, Roots, Stems and Branches (RSB) and First Serenade, all from Dominica.

Read more : History of bouyon

What is lapo kabwit?

Lapo kabwit is a traditional Dominican drum made with goat skin, central to chante mas and jing ping. Its rhythms drive classic bouyon percussion.

Read more : Bouyon instruments

What is chante mas?

Chante mas is Dominica's carnival masquerade song tradition, sung in Kweyol with lapo kabwit drums. It feeds bouyon's call and response party energy.

Read more : History of bouyon

What traditional dances are inside bouyon?

Bouyon blends the bele, the quadrille, the mazurka and the heel and toe polka traditions of Dominica with jing ping and cadence-lypso.

Read more : History of bouyon

How did bouyon reach Guadeloupe?

Bouyon travelled from Dominica to Guadeloupe through touring bands, carnival exchanges and the shared Kweyol language, and Guadeloupe built its own harder scene, bouyon gwada.

Read more : Bouyon in Guadeloupe

What is the history of the World Creole Music Festival?

The World Creole Music Festival (WCMF) in Roseau is Dominica's flagship festival, three nights of Creole music at Windsor Park that anchor the island's independence season and showcase bouyon at home.

Read more : WCMF context

Did bouyon come from cadence-lypso?

Yes, in part. Bouyon is what happened when WCK plugged cadence-lypso and jing ping into modern keyboards and drum machines at the end of the 1980s.

Read more : Cadence-lypso

Styles and comparisons

What is bouyon soca?

Bouyon soca is the fusion of Dominica's bouyon with Trinidad soca. The term was popularised by producers outside Dominica, mainly in Saint Lucia, blending 1990s bouyon rhythms with soca songwriting.

Read more : Bouyon soca

What is bouyon gwada?

Bouyon gwada is Guadeloupe's own bouyon scene: a harder, faster local evolution of the Dominican sound, in French and Creole, built for Gwada sound systems and carnival.

Read more : Bouyon gwada

What is shatta bouyon?

Shatta bouyon is the fusion of Martinique's shatta dancehall with Dominica's bouyon: shatta vocals and bounce over bouyon riddims.

Read more : Shatta bouyon

What is bouyon-muffin?

Bouyon-muffin is a fusion genre mixing bouyon with ragga and dancehall vocals, one of bouyon's first offshoots listed alongside reketeng.

Read more : Bouyon-muffin

What is reketeng?

Reketeng is a bouyon offshoot, a raw street variant of the genre that grew from Dominica's sound system and fete culture.

Read more : Reketeng

What is old school bouyon?

Old school bouyon is the original 1990s sound of WCK, RSB and First Serenade: live band energy, jing ping and cadence roots, and the first electronic riddims.

Read more : Old school bouyon

What is gospel bouyon?

Gospel bouyon carries bouyon rhythms into church: the same drums and drive, with praise and worship lyrics in Kweyol and English.

Read more : Gospel bouyon

What is modern bouyon?

Modern bouyon is today's production style: programmed drums, synths and samples, trap and dancehall influences, built by a new generation of producers in Dominica and the French Antilles.

Read more : Modern bouyon

What is the difference between bouyon and zouk?

Zouk is the French Antilles' smoother, romantic dance music from the 1980s. Bouyon is faster and rawer, a carnival jump up sound, though zouk is one of the styles cooked into bouyon.

Read more : Bouyon vs zouk

What is the difference between bouyon and shatta?

Shatta is Martinique's dancehall: sparse, heavy bass bounce for dance moves. Bouyon is Dominica's full band and riddim tradition, faster and denser. They fuse as shatta bouyon.

Read more : Bouyon vs shatta

Bouyon vs Dennery segment: are they the same?

They are cousins, not twins. Dennery segment is Saint Lucia's fast, raw party sound; bouyon is Dominica's genre with jing ping and cadence roots. The scenes trade artists and riddims constantly.

Read more : Bouyon in Saint Lucia

What is a bouyon riddim?

A riddim is the instrumental track several artists voice in turn. In bouyon, a hot riddim can carry a whole carnival season, with every singer bringing their own song to the same beat.

Read more : Bouyon riddim

What is instrumental bouyon?

Instrumental bouyon is the riddim without vocals: pure drums, bass and keys, used by DJs, dancers and producers, and searched by fans who want the beat alone.

Read more : Instrumental bouyon

What is a bouyon remix?

A bouyon remix flips an existing song, Afrobeats, pop, rap or R&B, onto a bouyon riddim. Smokiller's Bouyon Shattaz remixes are a leading example of the format.

Making bouyon

How do you make a bouyon beat?

Start from the riddim: fast drums inspired by lapo kabwit and jing ping patterns, a heavy bassline, bright keyboard riffs and space for call and response vocals in Kweyol.

How fast is bouyon music (BPM)?

Bouyon is a fast genre and tempo is part of its identity: it is built for dance and carnival. Producer HYPNO's track Mauvais Plan, for example, runs at 160 bpm.

Read more : Bouyon BPM

What is a bouyon drum kit?

A bouyon drum kit is a producer pack of the genre's drums: lapo kabwit style kicks and percussion, snares, shakers and riddim loops for building bouyon beats in a DAW.

What is a bouyon type beat?

A bouyon type beat is an instrumental made in the bouyon style for artists to voice, sold and shared by producers as type beats in the bouyon BPM and drum language.

What instruments are used in bouyon music?

Traditional bouyon uses lapo kabwit goat-skin drums, tambour bele, accordion, shak shak (maracas) and boom boom, layered today with keyboards, drum machines and synths.
